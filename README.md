# Overseas Filipino Workers 2025 Remittance Analysis

This report analyzed 4,215 survey records from PSA, which represent about 3.03 million overseas Filipinos once survey weights are applied. It moved from exploratory data analysis (EDA) to five analytical angles: remittance behavior, remittance-to-income, persona segmentation, return and reintegration, and regional supply. All population figures are weighted with the survey weight (RSWGT) unless marked as sample counts.

## Headline Findings
| What we found | In numbers |
|---|---|
| Almost every working OFW sends money home | **8 in 10** (80.7%) of workers send cash home |
| A typical amount sent | About **₱90,000** over six months |
| Most of the money is spent, not saved | **75%** goes to daily needs; only **14%** is saved and **6%** invested |
| Many families save or invest nothing | **4 in 10** (40.5%) spend all of it on daily needs |
| Banks and money transfer shops are about equal | Banks 48%, transfer services 49% |
| Lower earners give up the most | Those earning ₱20,000 or less a month send **half** of their income |
| A large share have already come home | **38%** have returned |
| A few regions supply most workers | CALABARZON, NCR and Central Luzon provide **48%** |

---

## Why This Matters

Remittances support millions of Filipino households. Understanding **who sends, how, and what the money is used for** helps banks, remittance companies, government agencies and community groups design better services: cheaper ways to send money, easier ways to save, and support for workers who return home.

---

## About the Data

- **4,215 people** answered the survey. Using the PSA's weighting, they stand in for about **3.03 million** overseas Filipinos.
- Some of them have already returned home.
- Not everyone worked abroad (about 23% did not), so questions about income and remittances are blank for them. That is expected and not a mistake.

## Finding 2: How Much of Their Pay Do They Send Home?

The survey does not say whether the remittance amount covers one month or longer. Using one month gives impossible results (most people would be sending more than they earn), so we assumed **six months**. **This is an assumption and should be confirmed with PSA.**

**Result:** The typical worker sends about **39% of their income** home.

| Who sends the biggest share of income? | Biggest | Smallest |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly pay | Earning ₱20,000 or less: **50%** | Earning ₱50,000 or more: about **30%** |
| Job | Basic jobs: 44% | Clerical: 20%, managers: 22% |
| Where they work | Kuwait: **50%**, Singapore: 45%, Saudi Arabia: 43% | Americas and Europe: 30% |
| Education | High school: 44% | College graduates: 31% |
| Marital status | Married: 43% | Single: 28% |
| Age | 45 to 54: 42% | 24 and below: 28% |
| Work type | Land-based: 40% | Sea-based: 31% |
| Sex | Women: 40% | Men: 36% |

**Takeaway:** The less people earn, the bigger the share they send home. Lower-paid workers carry the heaviest family-support load, even though their pesos are fewer.

## Finding 3: Four Types of Senders

We grouped remitters by how they behave (how much they earn, send, save and invest, and how long they have been abroad). The groups are **descriptive labels, not hard categories**: they overlap somewhat.

| Type | Share of senders | Typical pay | Typical amount sent | Months abroad | Age | Spent / saved / invested | Who they are |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Newer mid-income senders** | 40% | ₱30,000 | ₱60,000 | 16 | 34 | 80% / 13% / 2% | Mostly women, Saudi Arabia, basic jobs |
| **Long-stay, spend-first** | 30% | ₱30,000 | ₱78,000 | 40 | 43 | 86% / 6% / 2% | Mostly women, Saudi Arabia, basic jobs |
| **High earners who save** | 22% | ₱90,000 | ₱200,000 | 27 | 41 | 63% / 27% / 6% | Mostly men, Americas, machine operators |
| **Investor-minded** | 7% | ₱30,000 | ₱90,000 | 22 | 37 | 42% / 10% / 46% | Mostly women, Saudi Arabia, basic jobs |

**What stands out:**
- The investor-minded group earns the **same pay** as two other groups but puts **46%** into investments. What people do with money differs more than what they earn.
- Among mid-income workers, savings fall the longer they stay abroad (13% at 16 months, 6% at 40 months). This is worth studying further.

## Finding 4: Coming Home

**38% of OFWs have already returned**, and 20% came back between April and September 2025. The typical person still abroad expects to stay about two more years.

> Note: only 44% of returnees actually worked abroad. The group also includes short stays and tourists.

### Who comes home?

| Group | Share returned |
|---|---|
| Age 55 and above | 71% |
| Age 24 and below | 68% |
| Age 25 to 44 | about 30% |
| Seafarers | 38% |
| Land-based workers | 19% |
| Women / Men | 38% / 39% |
| Worked in Hong Kong or Singapore | 49% |
| Worked in Saudi Arabia, Kuwait or UAE | 22% to 24% |

### When do they come home?

| Time abroad | Share who returned |
|---|---|
| Under 1 year | 16.7% |
| 1 to 2 years | 18.1% |
| **2 to 3 years** | **30.6%** |
| 3 to 4 years | 23.5% |
| 4 to 5 years | 20.2% |

The peak at 2 to 3 years matches the standard two-year work contract.

### Why do they come home?

| Reason | Share |
|---|---|
| Other reasons (not specified in the survey) | 56.4% |
| End of contract | 35.9% |
| Visiting family | 7.4% |
| COVID repatriation | 0.3% |

Contract end is the reason for **90%** of returns from Kuwait and **80%** from Saudi Arabia, but only 12% to 19% from Japan, Taiwan and Hong Kong. Because "other reasons" is the biggest answer, we should be careful about guessing motives.

Returnees earned about the same as those still abroad (₱35,000 a month), but only 20% reported sending money home, compared with 89% of those still abroad. This reflects timing, because the question was about the survey period.

|---|---|---|---|
| CALABARZON | 21.0% | 58% | Saudi Arabia |
| NCR (Metro Manila) | 17.1% | 56% | Americas |
| Central Luzon | 10.0% | 53% | Saudi Arabia |
| Ilocos | 7.4% | 64% | Americas |
| Western Visayas | 6.5% | 59% | Europe |
| Central Visayas | 5.2% | 41% | Saudi Arabia |
| Cagayan Valley | 5.1% | 66% | Saudi Arabia |

| SOCCSKSARGEN | 3.7% | 79% | Saudi Arabia |

The top three regions supply **48%** of all OFWs; the top five supply **62%**.

**Regional highlights:**
- **Mindanao is a Saudi Arabia corridor.** Saudi Arabia is the top destination for 58% of OFWs from ARMM, 45% from SOCCSKSARGEN and 41% from Zamboanga. ARMM OFWs are 83% women and the youngest group.
- **Metro Manila OFWs are the oldest and best educated.** About 64% are college graduates.
- **Central and Western Visayas send the most seafarers.**
- **Basic jobs are the number one job in every region.**
- **City-based OFWs send more** (₱90,000 vs ₱72,000 for rural), but the share of income sent is about the same.

## Can We Predict Who Will Send Money?

Not really. Because nearly all working OFWs send money home, personal details like age, education or region explain very little about who does. Our prediction test scored 0.60 on a scale where 0.50 is a coin flip and 1.00 is perfect. **Products should therefore focus on *how* people send and use money, not *whether* they send it.**

## What We Recommend

| # | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Offer **low-cost digital money transfer with automatic savings** to workers in the Gulf and Hong Kong | They rely on transfer services, send 43% to 50% of pay and save only 10% to 14% |
| 2 | Offer **investment and savings products** to the "investor-minded" and "high earners who save" groups | They make up 29% of senders and are the most open to investing |
| 3 | **Time reintegration help to contract end** (savings advice, small business loans, job placement) | Contract end drives most returns from Kuwait, Saudi Arabia and sea-based work; the 2 to 3 year mark is the peak |
| 4 | **Focus outreach on CALABARZON, NCR and Central Luzon**, and make a separate plan for the Mindanao to Saudi corridor led through women's networks | These three regions are 48% of all OFWs |
| 5 | **Group people by how they behave, not just by how much they earn** | Mid-income workers look alike on pay but very different in how they spend |

## Things to Keep in Mind

- **The six-month assumption** for remittances is not confirmed. If the real period is 12 months, every "share of income" figure would be about half as large.
- **Small differences may not be meaningful.** The survey file does not allow us to calculate margins of error.
- **Self-reported and one-time.** People told the survey what they remember. We can see links (for example, savings dropping with time abroad) but cannot say one thing causes another.
- **Some groups are small** (for example managers, farm workers, and a few regions). Treat those numbers as rough hints.
- **The four sender types overlap.** They are useful labels, not sharp boundaries.
- **Return reasons are unclear,** since "other" is the largest answer.
- A few records (young children and people aged 70+) were kept in the analysis. Removing them would barely change results.

## For Technical Readers

**Method in brief:** Weighted statistics using PSA survey weights (`RSWGT`); weighted medians as the main summary; groups under 30 records excluded from comparisons; no zero-filling of missing amounts; K-means clustering (k = 4) on standardized log income, log remittance, months worked, savings share, investment share and age (silhouette about 0.2); weighted logistic regression with 5-fold cross-validation (AUC 0.60).
